THE QUESTION

Is the raw pay gap real — or explainable?

Raw pay-gap headlines are everywhere, but they rarely separate two very different stories:

Unequal pay — the same role, same experience, paid differently.

Unequal representation — different groups concentrated in different, differently-paid roles.

This project uses regression and hypothesis testing to find out which one is actually happening.

1

Why it matters

Pay-equity audits are becoming standard practice across Europe — companies need defensible, data-driven answers.

2

The approach

Move beyond a single average — test significance, then control for role, seniority, and department.

METHODOLOGY

From a single average to a defensible answer

1

Raw Gap

Compare mean pay, Male vs Female — no controls.

2

Significance Test

Welch's t-test — is the gap real, or noise?

3

Partial Regression

Control for Age + Seniority only.

4

Full Regression

Add Department, Job Title, Education.

5

Traffic-Light Score

Classify by p-value: significant or not.

Tools: Welch's t-test (unequal variance) · OLS via statsmodels · Random Forest cross-check via scikit-learn

FINDING 02  ·  THE TWIST

Controlling for age & seniority made it worse

p = 7.85 × 10⁻²⁴
still highly significant

Why would the gap grow?

Women in this dataset have, on average, slightly more favorable age and seniority profiles. That advantage was quietly masking the true gender-linked gap in the raw numbers.

Once we level the playing field on age and seniority, the underlying gap becomes fully visible — and larger.

08 / 13

FINDING 03  ·  THE ANSWER

Add role & department — the gap collapses

0
p = 0.28 · not significant

The verdict:

This is not unequal pay for equal work.

It's uneven representation — men and women are concentrated in different roles and departments that pay differently.
